INTERACTIVE EXPERIENCE RESEARCH

LUNI

Assessment 1

An exploration of interaction, discovery and visual expectation.

01 — VISUAL LANGUAGE

First impressions and perceived purpose

The first thing I noticed when entering Luni was its highly polished 3D modelling. The colour palette is very consistent, creating a visually comfortable experience without being overly intense. The combination of colour, typography, rendering and 3D objects reminded me of holographic or iridescent materials, which immediately gave the website a distinctive visual identity.

This visual language also influenced how I understood the purpose of the experience. Rather than seeing Luni as a conventional information website, I interpreted its primary goal as encouraging users to stay on the page and explore. The interface continuously responds to movement, while animated 3D objects remain active within each major section. Even when I was not directly interacting with an object, something was still moving and attracting my attention. This created a continuous sense that there might be something else worth discovering.

02 — EXPLORATION

How the interface encourages interaction

During my two-minute interaction, I initially moved my mouse around the page and scrolled through the different sections. I then began clicking on individual 3D models to test whether they were interactive. I also entered the Career and Luni Life sections, explored their content, and continued scrolling through text, visuals and photographs.

The action I repeated most often was clicking on different 3D models. I spent the most time in the sections containing these objects because they felt playful and encouraged experimentation. I was constantly trying to determine which objects could respond to me.

Interestingly, the website does not explicitly explain every possible interaction. Instead, it provides two broad ways to explore: clicking into different sections or scrolling through the experience. Within the sections, only a small number of explicit interaction cues, such as the “Discover” button, are provided. Other interactions are communicated through the appearance and movement of the 3D objects themselves. This ambiguity encourages users to experiment rather than simply follow instructions.

03 — DISCOVERY

Game-like references, curiosity and time

The experience reminded me of 3D exploration games such as Sky: Children of the Light and Superliminal. Like these experiences, Luni creates the feeling of entering a visual environment rather than simply navigating a webpage. This reference changed the way I behaved: instead of only reading the information, I wanted to move around, test objects and search for something hidden.

The experience therefore created a feeling similar to looking for an “easter egg”. At first, I felt curious and surprised when I discovered that some of the models could be interacted with. I also became interested in whether other objects might contain unexpected interactions.

However, I see Luni as a relatively short, one-time exploration. I would probably spend around thirty minutes discovering most of its interactive elements. After the main interactions have been found, there are fewer new things to attract my attention. The interactions are relatively simple and repetitive, while the limited amount of textual content means that there is not much reason to return repeatedly. My curiosity therefore gradually changed into boredom.

04 — FRICTION

When exploration becomes information

The most frustrating aspect of the experience was the contrast between its beautiful interactive environment and the way detailed information was presented.

The 3D environment created an expectation that I should continue exploring and looking for hidden interactions. However, when I entered some information sections, the experience shifted into a much more conventional linear format. Instead of discovering another interactive element, I encountered text that required me to stop exploring and read.

This created a conflict between two different modes of interaction. The visual design encouraged me to behave like a player exploring an environment, while some of the information design required me to behave like a reader navigating a conventional website. When I encountered larger blocks of text, I found myself wanting to skip them and continue searching for another interactive “easter egg”.

05 — SATISFACTION

What makes the experience memorable

Despite this limitation, the most satisfying part of Luni was still its combination of beautiful 3D modelling and interaction. Being able to click on the objects and discover how they responded made the website feel more like an environment than a traditional webpage.

The holographic and iridescent colour palette was also particularly memorable. The consistent visual treatment across the modelling, rendering and typography created a strong sense of identity and made the experience immediately appealing.

Ultimately, Luni was most successful for me when it allowed its visual language and interaction to work together. Its strongest quality was not the amount of information it provided, but the curiosity created by the possibility that there was always something else to discover.
